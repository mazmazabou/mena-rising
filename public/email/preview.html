Houthi attacks and Hormuz tanker strikes push Brent above $115 and rattle Gulf investor confidence

Security shocks, not fundamentals, set the week's agenda. Washington ordered embassy staff out of Saudi Arabia after near-daily Houthi attacks, Riyadh postponed a major investment conference, and Iran reported seven tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent at $115.22 (down 3.0% on the week after a six-month high) is a windfall for current-account surplus states such as the UAE (15.7% of GDP) and Qatar (14.8%). It is a growing burden for importers such as Egypt (14.1% inflation, EGP down 2.1% to 56.22) and Turkey (34.9% inflation). The divergence between hydrocarbon exporters and energy-importing, high-inflation economies is widening, and Gulf risk premia now matter more than headline growth of 4.5% in Saudi Arabia.

Risk radar

 
 

Critical risk

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia

Near-daily Houthi attacks have led the US to order embassy staff out of Saudi Arabia, and the UK is deploying jets to defend the kingdom. Riyadh has postponed a major investment conference, signalling direct damage to investor confidence in an economy that grew 4.5% in 2025.
 

High risk

Hormuz shipping and oil price shock

Iran says seven oil tankers were attacked near the Strait of Hormuz amid fears of a return to war. Brent is at $115.22, down 3.0% on the week but near a six-month high, and any further disruption to Gulf flows would hit Qatar, the UAE and Oman's export routes and raise import bills across the region.
 

Medium risk

Energy-importer inflation and currency stress

Egypt's pound weakened 2.1% to 56.22 per dollar with inflation at 14.1% and a current account deficit of 3.8% of GDP. Turkey's 34.9% inflation leaves little buffer against higher fuel costs, and Jordan's 5.3% of GDP deficit (2024) and 38.9% youth unemployment compound the social risks.
